# Autopsy: G-052, the dump-day short

*The strategy that printed t=5.1, survived an audit, and was making money that did not
exist. The flagship retraction of this ledger, written up in full because the mechanism is
the most instructive thing I own.*

## What it claimed

Short names that opened a dump day, intraday, exit by close. The backtest printed
**+50bps/day at t=5.1** across years of data. That is not a marginal result; that is the
kind of number that reorganizes your plans. It reorganized mine: within four working
sessions there were pro-forma projections and sizing plans built on top of it.

## The seduction

Everything about it looked disciplined. The hypothesis was mechanically plausible (forced
selling begets continuation). The sample was long. The t-stat was enormous. And it
**survived a first audit** — the standard checks for look-ahead in prices, for
survivorship, for cost realism all passed, because the leak was not in any of the usual
places.

## The bug

The entry signal, evaluated at **09:45**, used the day's **full-session volume** as a
filter input. Dump days have enormous volume — that is what a dump *is*. So the filter was
selecting days by information that only exists because the outcome it predicts occurred.
At 09:45 the signal knew the closing size of a day that had not happened yet.

The cruelty of this class of bug: it does not crash, it does not warn, and it loads the
sample with exactly the days the thesis wants to see. The better your hypothesis, the more
efficiently the leak flatters it.

## How it was caught

Not by re-reading the thesis — by rebuilding the input. A point-in-time reconstruction of
the volume field (only bars printed before 09:45) was run as a routine hygiene pass, not
because anything smelled wrong. The edge did not shrink. It **inverted**: the point-in-time
version loses ~15bps/day. Same idea, same names, same window; the only change was refusing
the future.

Its twin (G-053, the pump-day long, +64bps t=7.4 claimed) failed the same rebuild in the
same hour, landing at −48bps t=−11.9. One plumbing bug had manufactured two "independent"
five-sigma strategies pointing in opposite directions.

## The contamination map

Four working sessions of downstream research inherited the number before it died. All of
it was retracted the same day the rebuild landed. The retraction was published with the mechanism named, because a
retraction that hides its cause is just a deletion.

## What it minted

1. **Any field that can be caused by the outcome it predicts gets rebuilt point-in-time
   before the result is believed.** Volume, ranks, universe membership, "liquid names" —
   all of them can encode the future.
2. **A passed audit is a statement about the checks that ran, not about the strategy.**
   The first audit was competent and still missed it, because the checklist did not yet
   contain this failure mode. The checklist grows by autopsy.
3. **Speed of propagation is part of the cost.** Four sessions of downstream work is what a
   five-sigma number buys itself before anyone re-questions it. The bigger the print, the
   faster the quarantine has to be.

## Where it lives now

and every subsequent intraday row in this ledger carries a point-in-time-inputs line in its
verdict because of it.
